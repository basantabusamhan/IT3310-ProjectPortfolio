## Estimation Approach

I used a combination of bottom-up and analogous estimating because the RCHN project has defined activities and responsibilities, but exact labor costs and time requirements were not provided. I estimated the hours and rates for each role based on the work needed for development, testing, project management, and staff training. The limitation of this estimate is that the actual time and costs could change depending on project progress and additional work that may be needed.

**Labor Assumptions:**

**Assumption 1:** I estimated that the Project Manager would work 20 hours per week for 16 weeks. This should be enough time to coordinate the project and communicate with stakeholders. If more coordination or meetings are needed, the labor cost would increase.

**Assumption 2:** I estimated that the Developer would work 25 hours per week for 14 weeks. This allows time to develop the system and support data migration. If additional development or technical issues arise, more hours may be required, increasing the labor cost.

## Contingency Reserve

### Risk-Based Cost Exposure

- Risk 1: Workflow Differences Across Six Clinics — Potential Cost Exposure: $3,000. Differences in clinic workflows may require additional requirements gathering, system configuration, and development work.

- Risk 2: Staff Unavailable for Training or Pilot — Potential Cost Exposure: $2,500. Additional training sessions or scheduling adjustments may be needed if staff members are unavailable during the planned training period.

- Risk 4: Scheduling Defects Missed During Testing — Potential Cost Exposure: $3,000. Additional testing, troubleshooting, and development work may be required if scheduling problems are discovered later.

**Recommended Contingency Reserve:** $8,500

I recommend an $8,500 contingency reserve based on the potential costs of the identified risks. Workflow differences, staff availability, and scheduling defects could require additional time and resources. These risks could affect the project schedule and increase costs, so having a reserve would help NovaTech respond without immediately requesting additional funding from Marcus Webb.

## Budget Tradeoff Analysis

Before agreeing to reduce the budget by 10 percent, I would analyze the project expenses, staffing needs, schedule, and risks to determine where costs could be reduced without affecting the approved project outcome. I would look for unnecessary expenses first and make sure important project activities are not affected.

**Tradeoff or Risk 1:** Reducing development hours could delay the project or leave less time to fix technical issues and appointment scheduling problems.

**Tradeoff or Risk 2:** Reducing training or testing resources could leave employees unprepared to use the new system and increase the chance of problems after deployment.

I would try to reduce costs without sacrificing system quality, staff training, or data accuracy. If the reduction puts the project outcome at risk, I would explain those concerns to Marcus Webb before making any changes.
