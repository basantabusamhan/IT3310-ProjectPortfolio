**Owner:** Scrum Master  
**Method:** Pull

## Communication Method Justification

**Entry 1: Marcus Webb**

I chose push communication for Marcus Webb because he needs regular updates about project progress, major risks, and important decisions. Sending executive summaries directly to him makes sure he receives important information without having to search for it.

**Entry 4: RCHN IT Support Team**

I chose pull communication for IT Support because the team needs ongoing access to technical information and project updates. A shared work view allows them to check information whenever they need it.

## Escalation Path

### Level 1: NovaTech Project Team
**Role:** Project Manager

Handles issues that can be resolved within the project team, such as missed tasks, minor technical problems, and communication delays.

- **Expected response time:** Within one business day.
- **Trigger:** An issue cannot be resolved by the assigned team member or a project task is falling behind.

### Level 2: RCHN Clinic Leadership
**Role:** Clinic Managers and Chief Medical Officer

Handles issues that require client involvement, especially problems affecting clinic workflows, staff, or patient care.

- **Expected response time:** Within two business days.
- **Trigger:** An issue requires client input or cannot be resolved by the NovaTech team.

### Level 3: Executive Leadership
**Role:** Marcus Webb, Executive Sponsor

Handles major project decisions involving deployment delays, scope changes, budget concerns, or risks that could affect the overall project.

- **Expected response time:** Within one business day for critical issues.
- **Trigger:** An issue requires executive approval or could significantly affect project success.

*These response times are planning recommendations.*

### Escalation Scenario

If the appointment-data discrepancies identified in my Week 9 risk register materialized during testing, the escalation would unfold as follows:

**Step 1:** The Project Manager would work with the project team to investigate the discrepancies, document the affected records, and determine the cause.

**Step 2:** If the issue could not be resolved within one business day, the Project Manager would escalate it to the Clinic Managers and CMO because inaccurate appointment information could affect clinic operations and patient care.

**Step 3:** If the issue remained unresolved or affected deployment readiness, Marcus Webb would be notified and asked to make an executive decision about delaying deployment until the appointment data was verified.

## Gap Analysis

### Communication Gap

The gap I identified in my communication plan is that it does not clearly explain how urgent system or scheduling problems will be communicated to clinic staff. This matters because staff need to know when a problem could affect their daily work. To fix this, I would add an immediate notification process for issues affecting clinic operations, with the Project Manager responsible for sending updates.

### Silence as a Communication Decision

A situation where NovaTech silence could create a stakeholder problem is during system testing. Even if the project is on schedule, clinic managers might assume there are delays or unresolved problems if they do not receive updates. My communication plan prevents this by providing weekly status updates and communicating major changes as they happen.
